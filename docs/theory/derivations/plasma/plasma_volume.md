# Derivation of the $\mathrm{O}(\delta^2)$ plasma volume

:::{admonition} Source
:class: thesis-source

Adapted from T. Auclair, PhD thesis, Aix-Marseille Université (2026), Appendix B.3. The thesis describes D0FUS v2.7.0. Where the code has changed since, the {doc}`API reference </api/index>`, generated from the current sources, is authoritative.
:::


This page derives Eq. {eq}`eq:V_delta2`, the closed-form expression for the plasma volume that retains the leading-order correction in triangularity. The starting point is the Miller parameterisation, Eqs. {eq}`eq:miller_R` and {eq}`eq:miller_R`, with the simplifying assumption that $\kappa$ and $\delta$ are independent of $\rho$. This assumption is consistent with the use of Eq. {eq}`eq:V_delta2` as an Academic-mode fallback, where the radial profiles collapse to the constant edge values. All algebraic steps below were verified symbolically against `sympy`. The verification script is included in the manuscript source repository.

## Toroidal volume from the Miller Jacobian

The volume of a torus of revolution generated by an axisymmetric contour is given by Pappus’ theorem, $V = \int 2\pi R\, \mathrm{d}A$, where the integration is over the cross-section in the $(R, Z)$ plane. In the Miller parameterisation, the area element transforms as $\mathrm{d}A = |J_\mathrm{2D}|\, \mathrm{d}\rho\, \mathrm{d}\theta$, with the two-dimensional Jacobian

$$
J_\mathrm{2D} = \frac{\partial R}{\partial \rho}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial \theta} - \frac{\partial R}{\partial \theta}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial \rho}
$$ (eq:jacobian_def)

The volume therefore reads

$$
V = \int_0^1 \mathrm{d}\rho \int_0^{2\pi} 2\pi\, R(\rho, \theta)\, |J_\mathrm{2D}(\rho, \theta)|\, \mathrm{d}\theta
$$ (eq:V_full)

For constant $\kappa$ and $\delta$, with the shorthand $x \equiv \arcsin\delta$, the partial derivatives of the Miller coordinates are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial R}{\partial \rho}   &= a \cos(\theta + x \sin\theta) \\
	\frac{\partial R}{\partial \theta} &= -\rho\, a\, (1 + x \cos\theta) \sin(\theta + x \sin\theta) \\
	\frac{\partial Z}{\partial \rho}   &= \kappa\, a \sin\theta \\
	\frac{\partial Z}{\partial \theta} &= \kappa\, \rho\, a \cos\theta
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting into Eq. {eq}`eq:jacobian_def` gives

$$
J_\mathrm{2D} = \kappa\, \rho\, a^2 \left[ \cos\theta \cos(\theta + x \sin\theta) + (1 + x \cos\theta)\sin\theta \sin(\theta + x \sin\theta) \right]
$$ (eq:jacobian_intermediate)

For all shaping values used in conventional and high-field tokamak design ($|\delta| \leq 0.6$, hence $|x| \leq 0.64$), the Jacobian is positive throughout $[0, 2\pi)$ and the absolute value can be dropped.

## Taylor expansion to second order

For $|\delta| \lesssim 0.5$ the expansion $\arcsin\delta = \delta + \mathrm{O}(\delta^3)$ converges rapidly, so $x^2 = \delta^2 + \mathrm{O}(\delta^4)$ and one can keep $x$ as a formal expansion parameter, substituting $x \to \delta$ at the end. Expanding the trigonometric arguments to second order,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos(\theta + x\sin\theta) &= \cos\theta - x \sin^2\theta - \tfrac{x^2}{2} \sin^2\theta \cos\theta + \mathrm{O}(x^3) \\
	\sin(\theta + x\sin\theta) &= \sin\theta + x \sin\theta \cos\theta - \tfrac{x^2}{2} \sin^3\theta + \mathrm{O}(x^3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Inserting these into Eq. {eq}`eq:jacobian_intermediate` and collecting terms order by order in $x$ produces a Jacobian of the form $J_\mathrm{2D} = \kappa\rho a^2 [1 + x\, j_1(\theta) + x^2\, j_2(\theta) + \mathrm{O}(x^3)]$, with explicit but unenlightening expressions for $j_1$ and $j_2$. Multiplying by $R = R_0 + \rho a \bigl[\cos\theta - x \sin^2\theta + \mathrm{O}(x^2)\bigr]$ yields an integrand whose poloidal integral reduces to a few elementary trigonometric moments.

## Poloidal integration

After integration over $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ using the standard moments

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \cos^p\theta\, \sin^q\theta\, \mathrm{d}\theta
$$

the inner integral collapses to the compact expression

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} R\, J_\mathrm{2D}\, \mathrm{d}\theta = -\frac{\pi\, a^2 \kappa \rho}{4}\Bigl(R_0\, x^2 - 8 R_0 + 3\, a\, \rho\, x\Bigr) + \mathrm{O}(x^3)
$$ (eq:inner_integral)

Three observations are worth making at this point. First, the leading term $2\pi a^2 \kappa \rho R_0$ corresponds to the cylindrical-torus result and reproduces the Wesson formula upon further integration. Second, the linear-in-$x$ contribution to Eq. {eq}`eq:inner_integral` does *not* vanish: it scales as $\rho^2$ (rather than $\rho$ as the leading term) and originates from the cross-product between the $\cos\theta$ piece of $R$ and the $j_1(\theta)$ piece of the Jacobian, after taking the moment $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta\sin^2\theta\, \mathrm{d}\theta = \pi/4$. Third, the second-order-in-$x$ contribution scales linearly in $\rho$ and stems from the symmetric combination of expansion terms in both factors.

## Radial integration

Integrating Eq. {eq}`eq:inner_integral` from $\rho = 0$ to $\rho = 1$ uses $\int_0^1 \rho\, \mathrm{d}\rho = 1/2$ and $\int_0^1 \rho^2\, \mathrm{d}\rho = 1/3$. After multiplying by the toroidal factor $2\pi$, the volume reads

$$
V = -\frac{\pi^2 a^2 \kappa}{4}\bigl(R_0\, x^2 - 8 R_0 + 2\, a\, x\bigr) + \mathrm{O}(x^3)
$$

which can be rewritten as

$$
V = 2\pi^2 R_0\, \kappa\, a^2 \left(1 - \frac{a\, x}{4 R_0} - \frac{x^2}{8}\right) + \mathrm{O}(x^3)
$$

Substituting $x = \delta + \mathrm{O}(\delta^3)$ and absorbing the cubic remainder gives the result quoted in the main text,

$$
\boxed{\;V_{\mathrm{O}(\delta^2)} = 2\pi^2 R_0\, \kappa\, a^2 \left(1 - \frac{a \delta}{4 R_0} - \frac{\delta^2}{8}\right) + \mathrm{O}(\delta^3).\;}
$$ (eq:V_delta2_appendix)

The sign of the linear correction is the one that matters physically. A positive triangularity ($\delta > 0$) contracts the plasma volume relative to the cylindrical-torus reference by an amount that scales as the inverse aspect ratio $a/R_0$. The quadratic term is symmetric under $\delta \to -\delta$ and contributes a smaller, always-negative correction. For the ITER baseline ($a/R_0 = 0.323$, $\delta = 0.50$, $\kappa = 1.85$), the linear correction amounts to $-4.0\%$ and the quadratic correction to $-3.1\%$, summing to a $-7.1\%$ deviation from $V_\mathrm{Wesson}$. Direct numerical integration of the Miller Jacobian on a $500 \times 200$ grid in $(\rho, \theta)$ gives $-7.2\%$, confirming the expansion at the $0.1\%$ level. Higher-order terms scale as $(a/R_0)\,\delta^2$ and $\delta^4$, both of order $10^{-3}$ for ITER shaping, indistinguishable from the trapezoidal integration error in the numerical Jacobian.

## Limits and consistency checks

Two limits provide sanity checks on Eq. {eq}`eq:V_delta2_appendix`. Setting $\delta = 0$ recovers the Wesson formula $V = 2\pi^2 R_0\, \kappa\, a^2$, which matches the Academic-mode closed form, Eq. {eq}`eq:V_acad`. The cylindrical limit $a/R_0 \to 0$ at fixed $\delta$ leaves

$$
V \to 2\pi^2 R_0\, \kappa\, a^2 \left(1 - \frac{\delta^2}{8}\right)
$$

which captures the residual cross-section reduction that triangularity imposes even on a straight plasma column. The linear $a\delta/R_0$ term vanishes in this limit, as expected from its toroidal origin: the linear correction tracks the asymmetry between the high-field side and the low-field side, which disappears when the curvature radius $R_0$ becomes infinite.

The same expansion technique can be applied to derive the $\mathrm{O}(\delta^2)$ first-wall surface area, but the algebra is more involved because the surface integrand $\sqrt{(\partial_\theta R)^2 + (\partial_\theta Z)^2}$ requires a square-root expansion, and no closed form as compact as Eq. {eq}`eq:V_delta2_appendix` emerges. In D0FUS, the surface integral is therefore left to the numerical contour evaluation of Eq. {eq}`eq:SFW_miller`.

### Impact of the peaking and poloidal maps

The more peaked the profiles, the more these integrated quantities deviate from the flat-plasma approximation. The fusion power is particularly sensitive to temperature peaking because the DT reactivity $\langle \sigma v \rangle$ rises steeply with $T$ in the 10 to 20 keV range, the bootstrap current depends on logarithmic pressure gradients, which peak sharply at the pedestal top in H-mode. These profile effects are the primary motivation for retaining a radial integration capability in a code that is otherwise 0D.

Mapping these profiles back onto the Miller flux surfaces gives the two-dimensional temperature and density distributions of {numref}`Fig. %s <fig:nT_maps>`, generated automatically by the Run mode. They are a convenient visual summary of the combined effect of the shaping and the profile ansatz, and make the pedestal structure of the H-mode density and temperature immediately apparent at the plasma edge.
